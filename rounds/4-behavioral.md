# Round 4: Behavioral (cross-functional, bar raiser, project deep dive)

## What it really tests
For individual contributors, three things above all:
- **Influence without authority.** You changed a decision you didn't own, with data and persuasion.
- **Ownership.** You did the work, made the call, and owned the result, including when it went wrong.
- **Handling ambiguity.** You made progress when the question, the data, or the goal was unclear.

Every good story has four parts the interviewer is listening for: **real tension, your action, a decision, a number.**

## How people fail
What hiring managers see most often:
- **Vague conflict.** "We had different opinions, but we talked it through." There was no tension, so there's no story.
- **"We" instead of "I".** The interviewer can't tell what you did.
- **No result.** The story ends with the analysis, not with what changed.
- **A story you can't defend.** It falls apart on the second follow-up ("what did the PM say, exactly?", "what would you do differently?").
- **Humble-brag failures.** "My weakness is that I care too much." Interviewers stop listening.
- **Too long.** Four minutes of setup, thirty seconds of action.

## Common questions
1. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a stakeholder. How did it end?
2. Tell me about a time you influenced a decision without having authority.
3. Describe a time the data said something your stakeholders didn't want to hear.
4. Tell me about a time you got something wrong. What did you change afterward?
5. Tell me about a time you had to work with unclear requirements or messy data.
6. Tell me about a project you owned end to end. (Expect a deep dive: 20 minutes of follow-ups.)
7. Describe a time you pushed back on a deadline or scope.
8. Tell me about explaining a complex result to a non-technical audience.
9. Tell me about a time you did something nobody asked you to do.
10. Tell me about a time you had to learn something quickly.
11. What's the most important feedback you've received?

## Role variants
| Role | What they probe |
|---|---|
| Data Scientist | defending a method, a result nobody liked, rigor vs speed |
| Data Analyst | reframing a request, a metric disagreement, influencing a PM |
| Product Manager | a launch trade-off, saying no, aligning engineering and design |
| Finance Analyst | delivering bad numbers to leaders, a forecast miss, pushing back on a budget |

**Manager add-on.** Expect these on top of the IC questions:
- Tell me about a hire you're proud of, and one that didn't work out.
- How did you handle someone who was underperforming?
- How do you decide what your team says no to?
- Tell me about defending your team's work or headcount to leadership.
- How have you grown someone on your team?

## A simple frame: Headline, Tension, Action, Result, Lesson
1. **Headline (1 sentence).** The result first. "I stopped us from shipping a false positive by holding a test to its full two weeks."
2. **Tension (2 to 3 sentences).** Who wanted what, and why it mattered.
3. **Action (the longest part).** What you did, said, and decided. "I", not "we". Include your actual words if you can.
4. **Result (1 to 2 sentences).** What changed, with a number.
5. **Lesson (1 sentence).** What you'd do again or differently.

Aim for about 2 minutes. Then stop. The follow-ups are where the real scoring happens.

## Rubric
**3/10 sounds like:** "We had a PM who wanted to end a test early. We talked about it and decided to wait. It worked out." No tension, "we", no number, no lesson.

**7/10 sounds like:** "A PM wanted to call a test after 4 days. I told them 4 days didn't include a full weekend, and Sunday is our biggest order day. I asked for the full 2 weeks. It ended flat, so we didn't ship a change that did nothing." Real tension, clear action, a result.

**9/10 sounds like:** the 7, plus they handle the follow-up ("what if they'd shipped anyway?") with a specific answer, plus a lesson that shows how they work now: "Since then I agree the test length with the PM before launch, so there's nothing to argue about on day 4."
