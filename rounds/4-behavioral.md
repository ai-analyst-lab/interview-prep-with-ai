# Round 4: Behavioral (cross-functional, hiring manager, values, project deep dive)

## What it really tests
For individual contributors, three things above all:
- **Influence without authority.** You changed a decision you didn't own, with data and persuasion.
- **Ownership.** You did the work, made the call, and owned the result, including when it went wrong.
- **Handling ambiguity.** You made progress when the question, the data, or the goal was unclear.

Under all three sits one test: **is your own experience in the answer?** Could another candidate say it word for word? If yes, it scores low, however polished it sounds.

Every good story has four parts the interviewer is listening for: **real tension, your action, a decision, a number.**

## Put yourself in every answer
- **"Why this company?"** has three parts:
  1. **Your own connection.** You use the product, or you believe in its purpose. One real moment.
  2. **One specific observation** from using it.
  3. **A story from your experience** that fits their problem, with a number.

  Use the product before the call. If you don't love the product, name the problem or purpose you do care about. Never fake it.
- **Every other question: tell a time it happened.** Even "how do you think about..." and "what would you do if..." questions. A person and an outcome first, then the principle.
- **Ease in, then get to the point.**
  - Smile, and use the interviewer's name.
  - Open with a few warm, natural words, not a rehearsed joke.
  - Then give the headline straight away.
  - Close warm: thank them, and ask one real question back.

## The question map
Eight types, paraphrased from patterns in recent data science, analytics, and product interview loops. Real interviewers mix them, and the follow-up is often the real question.

| Type | What it really tests | How it's asked | Most common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Why here, why now** | Real motivation, research, fit | What draws you to us? What are you looking for next? What would the ideal role look like? What have you been doing lately? | A mission-statement answer. Leading with perks or "I'm exploring". Not having used the product. A tangled gap story |
| **2. Impact and project deep dive** | Ownership, judgment, numbers that hold up | Walk me through your most impactful project. What are you proudest of? A measurement system you built: what decision did it change? *Follow-ups:* how much was realized vs projected? Why this approach over the alternative? | Payoff at minute four. The headline number falls apart under "how did you measure that?", or changes each time you tell it |
| **3. Conflict with a partner** | Disagreeing well, owning your part | Tension with a partner team? Your hardest PM or stakeholder, and what made it hard? *Asked twice:* where exactly was the tension? | "There wasn't really conflict." The same soft answer the second time |
| **4. Data nobody wanted to hear** | Influence, conviction, handling a loss | You were sure you were right, but the org wouldn't accept it. The data and a team's strategy disagreed. You pushed back on a decision that hurt the customer. | Rescuing the ending ("but we shipped it") when they want a real loss |
| **5. Failure and learning** | Self-awareness, a lesson that changed how you work | A setback: what happened, how could you have avoided it? If you could rewind, what would you change? *Follow-up:* how does it change how you work today? | A humble-brag, or a failure that was someone else's. A lesson with no habit attached |
| **6. "What do you do when..."** | Prioritizing and holding a line under pressure | Results wanted on day 7 of a 30-day test. Five teams want readouts at once. Engineering timelines don't match yours. A senior leader needs something today. Planned work vs requests. | A philosophy instead of a time it happened. Getting asked "can you give an example?" |
| **7. Ambiguity** | Making a call without all the facts | A consequential decision without enough information. Direction unclear or changing. Something you did that nobody asked you to do. | Principles only. No person, no outcome, no call |
| **8. AI in your work** | When you trust AI and how you check it | How do you use AI today? A time AI did something non-obvious: what broke, and how did you know it was good? | "Yes, a lot." No verification step, no before-and-after |

## How people fail, across every type
1. **Principle before example.** Answer with a person and an outcome before a principle.
2. **No numbers.** A whole round can pass without one.
3. **Dodging the harder version.** When a question comes twice, your first answer missed. Give the harder version.
4. **Too long, payoff last.** Three or four minutes, result at the end.
5. **Numbers that wobble.** Say each number the same way every time, and know how it was measured.
6. **"We" for work you led.** Be accurate, not modest.

Also watch for:
- **Humble-brag failures**, such as "My weakness is that I care too much."
- **A story you can't defend** on the second follow-up.

## Answer shapes
| Shape | Use it for | How it goes |
|---|---|---|
| **Headline + number first** | Default, every question | The result in one sentence, with a number. Then the story |
| **STAR, inside the story** | Keeping the middle in order | Situation, Task, Action, Result, after the headline |
| **Stakes, Conflict, Call, Number** | Conflict, influence, leadership | What was at risk. Who disagreed and why. The call you made. What it moved |

**The 90-second rule.** Give a complete answer in about 90 seconds, then stop. The follow-ups are where the real scoring happens.

## Role variants
| Role | What they probe |
|---|---|
| Data Scientist | defending a method, a result nobody liked, rigor vs speed |
| Data Analyst | reframing a request, a metric disagreement, influencing a PM |
| Product Manager | a launch trade-off, saying no, aligning engineering and design |
| Finance Analyst | delivering bad numbers to leaders, a forecast miss, pushing back on a budget |

## Manager add-on: leading people
Expect these on top of the IC questions:
- A report you inherited who wasn't performing: how you diagnosed it, what you did, the outcome, and what you'd do sooner.
- Someone who grew a lot under you, over about a year.
- A hire from scoping the role to their 90-day mark.
- A team where nobody gave each other feedback. What do you do in your first 50 days?
- Your top three principles as a leader.
- When you step in and do the hands-on work yourself.
- How you decide what your team says no to.

**Most common failures:**
- Picking a case that was hard for outside reasons (legal, a reorg) instead of one that tested your judgment.
- Not quoting the actual feedback you gave.
- Principles with no person attached.

Run [kickoff session 5](../kickoff/session-5-managers.md) for these.

## Rubric
**3/10 sounds like:** "We had a PM who wanted to end a test early. We talked about it and decided to wait. It worked out." No tension, "we", no number, no lesson.

**7/10 sounds like:** "A PM wanted to call a test after 4 days. I told them 4 days didn't include a full weekend, and Sunday is our biggest order day. I asked for the full 2 weeks. It ended flat, so we didn't ship a change that did nothing." Real tension, clear action, a result.

**9/10 sounds like:** the 7, plus:
- They handle the follow-up ("what if they'd shipped anyway?") with a specific answer.
- The lesson shows how they work now: "Since then I agree the test length with the PM before launch, so there's nothing to argue about on day 4."
- It has a detail only they could say.
