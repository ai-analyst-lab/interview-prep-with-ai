# Round 1: Screen (recruiter and hiring manager)

## What it really tests
- **Fit:** does your background match the role they are hiring for?
- **Level:** does your scope sound like the level on the job posting?
- **Scope:** what you own, how big it is, and who depends on you.
- **Why you:** a clear, believable reason for this role and this move.

The screen is short and mostly standard questions. That makes it the easiest round to prepare for and the easiest to lose by improvising.

## How people fail
What hiring managers see most often:
- **Underselling.** "I support the growth team with analytics and help with their experiments" when the truth is "I own the growth team's activation metrics and designed the pricing test that moved annual plans from 9% to 14%." The recruiter writes down the smaller version, and that's the level you get slotted at.
- **Composing answers live.** Rambling through your background, starting at college, and getting to your current job at minute four.
- **No numbers.** "I work on a big product" instead of "a subscription app with about 2 million monthly users."
- **A weak "why".** "I'm looking for growth" says nothing. "I want to own experimentation end to end, and this team runs a lot of tests" says something.
- **Answering comp and "where else" off the cuff.** These have a right way to answer. Decide it once.

## Common questions
1. Walk me through your background.
2. What do you own today?
3. How hands-on are you? How much SQL or Python do you write in a week?
4. How do you use AI in your work?
5. Why are you looking?
6. Why this role, and why this company?
7. What's a project you're proud of? (the 2-minute version)
8. Who are your main stakeholders, and how do you work with them?
9. What kind of team or manager do you work best with?
10. What are your compensation expectations?
11. Where else are you interviewing, and what's your timeline?

Questions 1 to 5, 10, and 11 are the fixed answers in [coaching-rules.md](../interviewer/coaching-rules.md). Write them once in [me/fixed-answers.md](../me/fixed-answers.md).

## Role variants
The questions are the same for every role. What changes is the numbers you lead with.

| Role | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Data Scientist | the decisions your models or experiments drove, how many tests you run, the scale of the product |
| Data Analyst | the metrics you own, who reads your dashboards and how often, the decisions they drive |
| Product Manager | what you shipped, the metric it moved, the size of your product area |
| Finance Analyst | the budget or revenue you cover, forecast accuracy, the leaders you report to |
| Manager (add-on) | team size and mix, what the team owns, one person you grew |

## A simple frame: Now, Proof, Next
For "walk me through your background" and "what do you own":
1. **Now:** your current role and scope in one sentence, with a number.
2. **Proof:** one or two results that show the level, each with a number.
3. **Next:** why this role is the logical next step.

Keep it under 90 seconds. Stop and let them ask.

Example (Priya, fictional): "I'm a senior analyst at Fernly, a meal-kit subscription app, working with the growth and retention teams. Last year I traced a 12% drop in weekly orders to a broken iOS payment step within a day, and I designed a pricing test that moved annual plan uptake from 9% to 14%. I want to go deeper on experimentation, which is why this role caught my eye." The last sentence is `(suggested)`: it is not in her database yet. Her scope line is still Thin in her coverage map, so session 1 is her next kickoff.

## Rubric
**3/10 sounds like:** "So I started in consulting, then I moved to a startup, and now I'm at a meal-kit company where I mostly help the PMs with data. I'm looking for my next challenge." No scope, no number, underselling, no reason.

**7/10 sounds like:** "I'm a senior analyst on the growth team. I own the funnel metrics and run the experiment readouts, about 15 tests a year. I'm looking for a team where experimentation is central." Clear scope, a number, a reason. Missing a result.

**9/10 sounds like:** the 7, plus one result with a number, plus a "why this role" that connects to something specific about their team. Under 90 seconds. Sounds the same every time because it was written down first.
