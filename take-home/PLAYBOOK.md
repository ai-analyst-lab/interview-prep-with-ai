# Take-home playbook

A take-home is a case you do alone, with more time and less guidance. Most candidates lose points not on the analysis, but on scope, on decisions they can't explain, and on the presentation.

## Before you start (first hour)
1. **Lock the scope.** Write down, in two or three lines: the question you're answering, the decision it informs, and what you're not doing. If the prompt is ambiguous, email the recruiter with your reading and one clarifying question.
2. **Set a time budget** and keep to it. If they say "about 4 hours", a 20-hour submission can read as poor judgment, not effort.
3. **Start the decisions log** ([decisions-log.md](decisions-log.md)). Log every choice from here on.

## While you build
4. **Check the data first.** Row counts, grain, duplicates, missing values, date ranges. Write down what you found and what you did about it.
5. **Answer the question they asked.** Extra analyses are fine only after the main question has a clear answer.
6. **Sanity-check every headline number.** Does it reconcile to a total? Is it plausible?

## Before you submit
7. **Have a second AI review your work independently.** Open a fresh chat. Give it only the original prompt and your output, not your reasoning. Ask: "You're the hiring manager. What's the weakest claim here, and what would you ask me about it?" Fix what holds up.
8. **Write the risk list** ([risk-list.md](risk-list.md)). The three to five places an interviewer will push, each with your prepared response.
9. **Put the recommendation on the first page.** One sentence, with a number.

## In the presentation or follow-up call
10. **Answer, then reason, then depth only if asked.** Open with the recommendation. Give the two or three reasons. Stop. Let them pull you into the method.
11. **Own the limits.** "I assumed X because Y. If that's wrong, the answer changes like this." That scores higher than defending a weak spot.
12. **Use your decisions log.** When they ask "why did you do it this way?", you have the reason and the option you rejected.

## Using AI on a take-home
Follow the company's rules on AI use. If AI use is allowed, say how you used it and how you checked it. If you're unsure, ask the recruiter. Either way, you must be able to explain and defend every line.
