# Underselling audit

The most common screen mistake is describing your work as smaller than it is. Write out, or say out loud and transcribe, your answers to "walk me through your background" and "what do you own today", then paste them with your resume.

Use AI to prep, not during the interview. Follow each company's rules.

```
I'm preparing for interviews. Below are my resume and my answers to two screening questions: "Walk me through your background" and "What do you own today?" The answers are written out, or transcribed from me saying them out loud.

Audit my answers for underselling: places where I sound smaller than my resume says I am.

Rules
- Use only facts from my resume and my answers. Never invent scope, numbers, titles, or results.
- If a stronger version needs a fact I didn't give you, write [NEED NUMBER] or [NEED DETAIL].
- Do not use em dashes.

Output
1. Underselling lines: quote every line where I shrink my work ("just", "only", "helped with", "supported", "was part of the team", "small project", "not that technical"). Next to each, the resume line that says otherwise.
2. Left out: things on my resume I skipped that an interviewer at my target level would want to hear.
3. Rewrites: a true-but-stronger version of each answer, about 60 seconds spoken (about 150 words), result first, at least one number, "I" not "we".
4. One fix: the single change that would most raise how senior I sound.

Target role and level: [e.g. Senior Data Analyst]
My resume: [paste]
My answer to "walk me through your background": [paste]
My answer to "what do you own today": [paste]
```
