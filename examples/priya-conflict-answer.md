## The answer

Yeah, so, this was at Fernly. Fernly is a meal-kit company, so people subscribe and get boxes of ingredients every week, and the growth team is the team that works on getting more people to sign up and stick around. So there's a growth PM, there's a couple of engineers, there's design, and then there's analytics, which is where I sit, so we all kind of work together on the roadmap and the experiments and that sort of thing.

So at the time we were running a lot of tests on the checkout flow, because checkout is where a lot of people drop off, and the team had a new idea for how to show the delivery date earlier in the flow, which honestly was a pretty good idea, and design had done some nice work on it. So we set up the test, we picked the metrics together, it was the usual process, and we launched it.

And then a few days in, the early numbers looked really good. Like, the treatment was ahead, and everyone was pretty excited about it, which is understandable, because the team had been having kind of a slow quarter and this looked like a win. And there was a planning meeting coming up, and so there was a conversation about whether we could just call it and ship it, so it could go into the planning doc.

And so we talked about it as a team. There wasn't really a conflict, I'd say, it was more that we had different views on the timing. My view was that it was a bit early, because ordering at Fernly is really different on weekends, especially Sundays, so the first few days don't really tell you the full picture. And we kind of went back and forth on it, and in the end we agreed to let it run the full length.

And it actually came back flat. So the early lift basically went away once the weekends were in there. So we didn't ship it, which was the right call, because it wouldn't have done anything, and it would have taken engineering time to maintain.

And I think the team was fine with it in the end. It was a good learning for everyone, I think, about not reading too much into early results. And since then we've been a lot more careful with how we talk about early numbers, and we try to set expectations up front about how long a test needs to run. So yeah, I think it was a good example of working through a disagreement as a team and getting to the right answer together, and it's something I think about a lot when we launch new tests now.
