# My fixed answers

<!-- TEMPLATE: write these once, then practice them. Delete this line when done. -->

Every candidate gets asked these in some form. Don't compose them live. Write them here, practice them out loud until they sound natural, and the coach will flag it when you drift from your written version. Drill them with `/drill fixed answers`.

Keep each spoken answer under 90 seconds.

## 1. Walk me through your background.
**Tip:** Now, Proof, Next. Start with your current role and scope, then one or two results with numbers, then why this role. Don't start at college.

[your answer]

## 2. What do you own today?
**Tip:** Scope, team, metrics, who depends on you. Use "I own", not "I help with". This is the answer that sets your level.

[your answer]

## 3. How hands-on are you?
**Tip:** Be specific: which tools, how often, one recent example. "I write SQL most days, and last week I built the query behind our weekly retention readout."

[your answer]

## 4. How do you use AI in your work?
**Tip:** One concrete example with a result, plus how you check its output. Interviewers want judgment, not a tool list.

[your answer]

## 5. Why are you looking, and why this role?
**Tip:** Pull toward something, don't push away from your current job. Name one specific thing about this role or team.

[your answer]

## 6. What are your compensation expectations?
**Tip:** Decide your approach before the call. A common one is to ask for the role's range first, then say whether you're aligned. Research the market for your level so any number you give is one you chose, not one you guessed.

[your answer]

## 7. Where else are you interviewing?
**Tip:** Be honest and brief. Say what stage you're at and your timeline. You don't have to name companies.

[your answer]
